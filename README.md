# BU Campus Emergency App (Capstone UX Case Study)

A mobile app concept that helps Boston University students know exactly what to do in an emergency: who to call, where to go and what to take, step by step.

> **Team project.** This was my capstone (Jan–May 2026), built with teammates **Shiyun Wang** and **Catherine Liu** in collaboration with a client and project managers. The app is not finished. Our team delivered the research and prototypes, and another team has since taken over the project.

---

## The problem

BU students get BU Alerts by text and email, but an alert only says *that* something is happening, not *what to do about it*. The university's emergency protocols are public, but they live on the website as long pages that students rarely read ahead of time.

Our research found that in a real emergency, most students fall back on two options: run, or call 911. Few know the right steps for a specific situation, and there's no quick way to reach BU Police (BUPD) without looking up the number.

## Research

**User interview workshop.** We wrote an interview guide on how students experience and respond to campus emergencies, then interviewed students in rotating roles (interviewer, interviewee, recorder). Afterward, we clustered the notes into patterns and turned them into key insights:

- Students know BU Alerts exist, but they have limited familiarity with the actual emergency protocols.
- Students who had been through a real emergency valued real-time updates and clear instructions much more.
- Many prefer fast, direct actions (calling for help, location-based guidance) over reading information ahead of time.
- Students want resources they can use in the moment: clear navigation and fewer barriers to acting.

**Persona: Amanda Lee**, a 22-year-old engineering student at BU.
> "I want to know how to feel safe on my campus."

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Behaviors** | Gets BU Alerts; doesn't check emergency protocols; leaves the area when an alert comes in; shares her location with friends and family |
| **Goals** | Reach a fast-responding authority quickly; have first-aid guidance on hand |
| **Pain points** | Doesn't know what to do besides run or call 911; has no fast way to call BUPD without looking up the number |

**Journey map: a fire alarm goes off in Amanda's dorm.**

| Stage | What she does | What she's thinking | Feeling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Trigger | Wakes up to the alarm, checks her phone | "What is going on?" | Confused, panicked |
| 2. Information seeking | Opens BU Alerts, asks her roommate, texts friends in the building, searches where to go | "Where should I go? What should I do?" | Anxious |
| 3. Action | Grabs phone, laptop, wallet and backpack; takes the elevator down and goes out the door | "Am I overreacting? What if I can't get out?" | Stressed, uneasy |
| 4. Resolution | Waits for authorities, returns to the dorm | "Was everyone okay?" | Relieved |

The low point is step 2: she spends the most stressful minutes *searching* for information instead of acting on it. (She also takes the elevator during a fire, which is exactly the kind of mistake step-by-step guidance would prevent.)

## Design opportunities

- Centralized emergency information for campus, all in one place
- A map of emergency exits based on the user's location
- A simple checklist of what to grab
- Real-time updates
- Simple step-by-step instructions
- A one-tap button to call BUPD

## What we built

- **Protocol workflows.** We translated BU's [publicly available emergency protocols](https://www.bu.edu/safety/campus-safety/safety-response/) into step-by-step workflows for **seven emergency scenarios**.
- **Sitemap.** We mapped the app's structure and how each emergency scenario connects to its instructions.
- **Lo-fi to hi-fi prototypes in Figma.** We refined these through user research, client feedback and design presentations, and proposed navigation and notification enhancements along the way.

## How we'd measure success

- % of students who can find all the emergency info they need in **under 30 seconds**
- % of students who reach a safe exit within the recommended time
- % of students who follow the instructions correctly

## Status

This was a semester-long capstone. Our team handed off the research, workflows and prototypes, and another team is continuing the project. The app is a concept and prototype, not a released product.
